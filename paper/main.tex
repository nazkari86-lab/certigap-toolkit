\begin{abstract}
We study static search over ordered keys when only a limited number of
threshold comparisons may be materialized and the observed query distribution
may be contaminated. A CertiGap tree freely chooses at most $B$ prefix
comparisons and delegates every unresolved contiguous interval to a fixed
executable fallback. We give an exact Pareto-frontier dynamic program for any
deterministic per-key fallback profile, prove that a Huber-contamination model
reduces exactly to a convex combination of nominal average and worst-case
cost, and provide an independently checkable rational evaluator. We also give
an infinite family on which one-step greedy has unbounded additive error.
The implementation is cross-validated against a second exact recurrence,
brute force, and proof-carrying branch-and-bound on small instances. A
candidate-pruned C++ implementation and matched-budget microbenchmark evaluate
the quality, construction cost, lookup cost, and auxiliary memory of scalable
solutions. We report both wins and losses and distinguish mathematical,
empirical, and external-validation claims. AutoDRO additionally derives a
finite-sample total-variation radius from integer query counts, evaluates the
worst-case expectation of every generated candidate exactly, and automatically
selects budget, solver, and executable fallback under a memory constraint. On
proof-sized instances, a direct solver exhaustively enumerates the partial-tree
space and is globally optimal for the TV objective. For larger spaces, a
best-first anytime solver returns a feasible upper bound and independently
replayable lower bound after any declared expansion limit. We further extend
the model into Dynamic CertiRange, a complete point/range/update index with
depth caps, persistent snapshots, range-aware workload search, and replayable
structural artifacts.
Finally, Certified AutoIndex compiles a trace into an executable array,
Fenwick tree, segment tree, or one of two CertiRange variants under aggregate,
memory, depth, and snapshot constraints. Its omission-resistant artifact
proves minimum declared training cost over the complete fixed portfolio while
keeping chronological holdout evaluation outside the selection path.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem}
Let $a_1<\cdots<a_n$ be ordered keys with nominal access distribution
$\hat p$. A partial tree contains at most $B$ materialized threshold nodes.
If key $i$ reaches unresolved interval $[l,r]$ at depth $d$, a deterministic
fallback $F$ incurs $F(l,r,i)$ additional comparisons. Thus
\[
C_T^F(i)=d+F(l,r,i).
\]
For contamination level $\eta\in[0,1]$, we minimize
\[
J_\eta^F(T)=(1-\eta)\sum_i\hat p_iC_T^F(i)
             +\eta\max_i C_T^F(i).
\]

\section{Exact Dynamic Programming}
For every interval and remaining budget, the algorithm retains nondominated
pairs $(A,M)$ of nominal average and maximum cost. A fallback leaf contributes
\[
A_F(l,r)=\sum_{i=l}^r\hat p_iF(l,r,i),\qquad
M_F(l,r)=\max_{i=l}^r F(l,r,i).
\]
A split at $k$ contributes
\[
A=P(l,r)+A_L+A_R,\qquad M=1+\max(M_L,M_R).
\]
Structural induction shows that every feasible partial tree is enumerated.
Removing a state dominated in both coordinates is safe for every $\eta$.
Therefore minimizing $(1-\eta)A+\eta M$ over the root frontier is exact.

\section{Robustness}
If the true distribution is $p=(1-\eta)\hat p+\eta q$ for arbitrary $q$,
then
\[
\max_q\mathbb E_p[C_T^F]
=(1-\eta)\mathbb E_{\hat p}[C_T^F]+\eta\max_i C_T^F(i).
\]
Hence the objective is the exact robust risk for this contamination set.

\section{Automatic TV-DRO Selection}
Given integer query counts, AutoDRO constructs a smoothed nominal distribution.
It derives a conservative finite-alphabet total-variation radius from the
$L_1$ concentration inequality of Weissman et al.\ \cite{weissman2003} and
adds the exact TV distance introduced by smoothing. For fixed per-key execution
costs $c_i$, it evaluates
\[
\max_{q:\operatorname{TV}(q,\hat p)\leq\rho}\sum_i q_i c_i
\]
by transferring mass from the cheapest donor coordinates to the most
expensive receiver coordinates. An exchange argument proves this evaluation
exact. AutoDRO enumerates configured budgets, training robustness values,
solvers, and fallbacks, filters candidates by memory, and returns the minimum
verified portfolio score. This is an exact selection guarantee over the
generated portfolio, not over trees omitted by a heuristic portfolio.

\section{Direct TV-DRO Optimality}
For $s=0$, an interval has exactly one partial tree: its unresolved leaf. For
$s>0$, every valid tree has a unique root threshold $k$, a left tree with
$s_L$ splits, and a right tree with $s-1-s_L$ splits. Enumerating all legal
$k$ and $s_L$ and applying this recurrence therefore enumerates every feasible
ordered partial tree exactly once by induction on $s$. Combining this complete
enumeration with the exact fixed-tree TV evaluator proves that taking the
minimum score is globally optimal over the declared split and memory
constraints and configured fallback set. The implementation records counts by
split total and a SHA-256 digest of the enumerated tree space.

\section{Scalable Anytime TV-DRO}
A search state contains a partial tree and a canonical set of unresolved
leaves. Expanding the first unresolved leaf either closes it with the fallback
or enumerates every legal threshold split. These alternatives partition all
completions. For unresolved keys we assign only routing cost already incurred
and zero future fallback cost. This vector is componentwise no larger than
every completion, hence its worst-case TV expectation is a lower bound.

We strengthen it with two coding bounds. Every binary search code has nominal
mean depth at least $H(\hat p)$ and maximum depth at least
$\lceil\log_2 n\rceil$. For a partial state, each open interval contributes
its current routing depth plus the entropy of its conditional distribution,
while closed intervals contribute exact fallback cost. Taking the maximum of
these admissible bounds gives $L_s$ for state $s$. If $U$ is a feasible
incumbent, then
\[
\min\{U,\min_{s\in\mathcal F}L_s\}\leq OPT\leq U.
\]
A standalone verifier replays every best-first transition, recomputes bounds
and incumbents, reconstructs the remaining frontier, and validates the stop
condition and structural digest.

\section{Online Drift Certificate}
For $\delta=\operatorname{TV}(p,q)$ and a cost vector of range at most $R$,
$|\mathbb E_p c-\mathbb E_q c|\leq\delta R$. If a reference policy has
optimization gap $g$, applying this inequality to it and to the new optimum
gives current-distribution mean-cost regret at most
\[
g+2\delta R.
\]
This result supports a conservative horizon-aware rebuild trigger; it is not a
claim about unmeasured mutation or storage-engine latency.

\section{Dynamic CertiRange}
A partial routing tree is completed deterministically by midpoint subtrees.
A proposed routing split is retained only when both children can still be
completed inside a declared maximum depth $h$. The completed leaves therefore
form the singleton partition of the ordered keys and have height at most $h$.
Internal nodes store a monoid aggregate. Point updates path-copy the
root-to-key path, preserving old snapshot roots, while an inclusive range query
recurses only along its two boundary paths and visits $O(h)$ nodes.

For mixed workloads we directly replay point, update, and range records on
every completed candidate. The bounded range-aware beam retains balanced
completion as an incumbent, so it cannot return a higher training objective
than that included candidate. This is portfolio dominance rather than a
large-instance global optimum guarantee. A standalone artifact checker
recomputes topology, depth, aggregate state, mixed-trace objective, and
canonical digests.

\section{Relation To Prior Work}
Classical alphabetic and height-limited trees optimize a full tree
\cite{hutucker1971,larmore1994}. CertiGap instead limits the freely
materialized prefix and fixes all completion subtrees. Robust BSTs under
scenario and earth-mover uncertainty use different uncertainty sets and
solution classes \cite{angelopoulos2025,dinitz2024}. Learned indexes optimize
model-size and last-mile-search tradeoffs but generally do not provide this
split-budget exact program \cite{pgm2020,sosd2020}.

\section{Certified AutoIndex}
We evaluate a fixed ordered portfolio containing a contiguous array, Fenwick
tree, iterative segment tree, point-proxy CertiRange, and range-aware
CertiRange. For operation-cost vector $c$ and declared resource tuple
$(S,H,U)$, selection minimizes
\[
(1-\lambda)\operatorname{mean}(c)+\lambda\max(c)
  +\alpha S+\beta U
\]
over candidates satisfying aggregate, memory, depth, and persistent-snapshot
constraints. Positive backend-specific coefficients may calibrate each raw
visit type from target-system measurements. Ties are resolved by slots and
published portfolio position.

The artifact retains all five candidates, including rejected rows and reasons.
An independent verifier regenerates every topology, cost vector, resource
tuple, feasibility decision, and optional holdout score before checking the
winner. Thus the result certifies a portfolio minimum in declared primitive
visits. It neither proves global optimality over unlisted structures nor
equates structural visits with wall-clock latency.

For deployment, a strict versioned JSON compiler verifies the artifact before
emitting a deterministic C++17 header. Backend, aggregate, universe size, and
artifact digest are compile-time constants; a selected CertiRange additionally
embeds its complete checked topology. The reusable runtime supports point,
range, and update operations for sum, minimum, and maximum. Its snapshot is a
semantically isolated value-copy and therefore costs $O(n)$, unlike the
path-copy Python implementation.

For low-friction use, a separate single-header C++17 mode records normal
operations in-process and deterministically compares the same five backend
families. Range coverage is accumulated with a difference array, adaptive
topologies use depth-safe weighted splits, and reoptimization is an explicit
TV-drift-triggered call rather than hidden query work. This mode exposes the
complete leaderboard but deliberately omits the independent artifact replay
of the build-time compiler.

\section{Evidence And Limits}
The repository contains exact cross-validation, a standalone certificate
checker, synthetic and public-frequency workloads, temporal holdout, pruning
ablation, and C++ lookup measurements. Floating-point DP is not itself a
machine proof; the rational evaluator verifies submitted fixed-round trees.
The 120-row AutoDRO shift benchmark compares TV and nominal selection over an
identical tuned portfolio. TV selection records three wins, three losses, and
eighteen ties against nominal selection across 24 train/test pairs. A separate
181-case complete-space validation includes a strict witness where direct TV
optimization beats the Huber-generated portfolio. Across 3,000 deterministic
i.i.d. multinomial trials, empirical coverage of the inferred TV ball is at
least 0.996, while a separate sliding-window simulation reports the explicit
tradeoff between rebuild count and regret.
The anytime matrix matches complete tree-space optimization in 12 of 12 small
cases and contains 36 replay-verified trajectories for $n=16,32,64$.
Certified intervals are monotone with additional expansions; uniform
power-of-two cases close immediately through the entropy bound, while skewed
cases retain explicitly reported nonzero gaps. The C++ lookup benchmark also
includes read-only 80/20-hotspot and latest-biased YCSB-inspired
distributions, but is not presented as an official YCSB or RocksDB result.
Dynamic CertiRange matches complete routing-space optimization in 6 of 6 small
range-workload cases. In 36 scaling groups, range-aware search is never worse
than its included balanced candidate and strictly improves the former
point/endpoint proxy in 18 groups. Python and contiguous-node C++ mixed
benchmarks also show that Fenwick or iterative segment trees remain faster for
raw range sums; no blanket speed superiority is claimed.
The AutoIndex matrix contains 24 complete five-candidate portfolios (120
candidate rows) across four key-universe sizes, six temporal/constraint
scenarios, three aggregates/capability regimes, memory restrictions, and
mandatory snapshots. All 24 artifacts replay successfully. The selected
training candidate is always the portfolio minimum. Under explicit
point-to-range temporal drift, holdout regret can be nonzero and reaches the
reported matrix maximum; holdout is never used to revise the winner.
Across 24 compiler-integration cases, repeated generation produces
byte-identical headers and every source artifact independently verifies.
Cross-language executable tests cover all backend families, all three
aggregates, updates, and snapshot isolation; a separate CMake target compiles
and runs a generated CertiRange configuration.
The adaptive single-header matrix adds 24 native C++ cases over four sizes and
six workload/constraint modes; all return complete five-row leaderboards and
pass point/range/update/snapshot oracles. CMake installation and a downstream
\texttt{find\_package} consumer are executable tests.
External implementations, independent hardware reproduction, and a
domain-owner pilot remain external validation requirements.
